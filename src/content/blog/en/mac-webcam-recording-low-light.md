A Mac webcam can record at night when it produces a usable image in the available light. Its performance depends on the camera and room; ordinary webcams should not be assumed to provide infrared night vision. A [recent discussion about offline indoor cameras](https://www.reddit.com/r/homesecurity/comments/1xt6606/offline_camera_for_indoor_night_use/) raises the question of recording in a dark room. It concerns dedicated cameras, and one discussion does not establish a broader trend.

## Check the image before changing settings

Place the camera where it will actually be used and view the picture in Owl Cam at the expected time of night. Look for a dark or noisy image, bright lamps pointed into the lens, and reflections in windows. A small, steady room light may help a conventional webcam; check whether it illuminates the area you care about without shining into the lens. Camera capabilities vary, so check the device specifications and test your own setup.

## Test detection and playback

With the intended lighting, walk into the view, pause and leave. Review the saved recording from the beginning on the Mac. Try the same test at different distances and with the lights changing. Low light, distance and obstructions can affect motion and person detection; neither detection nor recording every event is guaranteed.

If changing shadows or exposure cause unwanted clips, adjust placement first and change one detection setting at a time. Repeat the same walk-through after each adjustment: reducing sensitivity can also cause missed events. See [our false-motion guide](/blog/reduce-false-motion-recordings).

## Know the limits before relying on it

Owl Cam works with built-in, USB and compatible Continuity cameras recognized by macOS; it does not directly support RTSP or ONVIF network cameras. The Mac must stay awake, the camera connected and the destination writable. It currently has no remote stream, phone app or push alerts. If a completely dark room or remote alerts are essential, check other equipment designed for those needs. See the [FAQ](/faq) for details.
